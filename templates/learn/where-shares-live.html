1. Two ways to hold Singapore shares

When you buy a Singapore share, it has to be recorded somewhere. There are two ways:

In a {{ term("cdp", "CDP account") }}, the shares sit at The Central Depository, which SGX runs, in your own name. In a {{ term("custodian", "custodian account") }}, your broker holds them for you, in the broker's name. Either way they're yours; what changes is who the company sees as the holder.

2. What changes for you

Dividends: with CDP they go straight into the bank account you link. With a custodian they land in your broker account first, and some brokers charge a fee each time.

Letters and votes: with CDP, company notices come to you and you can vote at meetings. With a custodian, voting usually goes through the broker, if it's offered at all.

Changing brokers: CDP shares stay put when you switch brokers. Moving custodian shares out can cost a transfer fee per stock.

Cost: custodian brokers are often cheaper per trade, which is why many beginners start with one. Neither is wrong; just know which one you have.

3. What if your broker goes bust?

Licensed brokers in Singapore must keep customers' shares apart from their own money, so your shares aren't the broker's to lose. But getting them back through a failed broker can take time. With CDP, they're already recorded in your name.

The broker being licensed by MAS matters far more than which account it uses. The next lesson shows how to check.
