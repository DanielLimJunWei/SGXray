1. The market is the bar to beat

You can buy the whole Singapore market in one go: the {{ term("sti-etf", "STI ETF") }} holds all 30 STI companies. It's an {{ term("index-fund", "index fund") }}: no picking, low cost, and exactly the market's return.

So every stock you pick yourself has to answer one question: did it do better than the same money in the STI ETF would have? If not, the effort and the extra risk didn't pay.

2. Most professionals don't beat it

S&P's SPIVA reports track this every year. In the 10 years to the end of 2024, at least 80% of US share funds run by full-time professionals did worse than their index after fees. Reports for other markets show similar results.

That doesn't mean picking is wrong, or that nobody can do it. It means beating the market is hard, and you should measure whether you are, instead of assuming.

3. Luck or skill?

A few good years can be luck. One great pick can carry a whole portfolio and hide that the others did worse. It takes many years, and looking stock by stock, to tell skill from a lucky streak.
