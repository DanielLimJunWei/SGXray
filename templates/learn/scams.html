1. What investment scams look like

They rarely look like scams. Common ones in Singapore: a chat group sharing "insider tips", a stranger who becomes a friend online and then shows you a trading app, an advert with a famous face, or someone pretending to be from a real bank or broker.

The giveaway is almost always the same: {{ term("guaranteed-return", "high returns with little or no risk") }}, often with pressure to act fast. Real investments go up and down. Nobody can guarantee a big, steady profit.

2. The check before you pay

Look the firm up in the {{ term("mas-directory", "MAS Financial Institutions Directory") }}. If it isn't there, or isn't allowed to do what it's offering, stop.

Contact it yourself through the phone number on its official website, not one from the chat or advert: scammers copy real firms' names and logos.

Never pay into a personal bank account, never share your Singpass or bank login, and be suspicious of any "fee" or "tax" you must pay before you can withdraw.

Not sure? Call the ScamShield helpline, 1799. It runs 24 hours a day.

3. If you've already paid

Call your bank straight away to try to stop the transfer, then make a police report. Speed matters: money that's still moving can sometimes be frozen.

{% endblock %} {% block question %}A chat group shares tips and a trading app where your balance grows 2% a week. To withdraw, you're told to pay a "tax" first. What's going on?{% endblock %} {% block answer %}

It's a scam, on three counts: steady guaranteed-looking growth, tips from a chat group, and a fee before you can withdraw. The "balance" is just a number on their screen. Don't pay the fee; call your bank and make a police report.
