1. The X-ray: one line, split open

A normal chart shows your portfolio's value going up or down. That line mixes three different things, so SGXray's X-ray chart splits it into layers:

{{ term("money-in", "Your own money") }} at the bottom: what you put in. It's not profit.

{{ term("price-gains", "Price gains") }} on top: what your shares have gained (or lost) in price.

{{ term("dividend", "Dividends") }} on top of that: the cash paid to you along the way.

The dashed line is the same money put into the STI ETF on the same days: the market, for comparison.

2. Why there are two returns

A {{ term("money-weighted", "money-weighted return") }} answers "how did my money do?" It counts when you added it: money added just before a rise helps, money added just before a fall hurts.

A {{ term("time-weighted", "time-weighted return") }} answers "how did my picks do?" It ignores when money went in, so it's the fair way to compare your choices with a fund or the market. When the two differ a lot, your timing made the difference.

3. Reading it calmly

SGXray uses end-of-day closing prices, not live ones. Where a number can't be worked out yet, it says why in words (like {{ term("pending") }}) and you can tap the word. Every table has a "What does this mean?" box, and a "Watch out" box when something deserves a second look.

Past returns don't predict future ones. The point of all this is understanding what happened, not guessing what's next.
