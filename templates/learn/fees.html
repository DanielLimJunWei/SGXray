1. What one trade costs

Every buy and every sell has three parts:

Your broker's {{ term("commission") }}: usually a small percentage of the trade, but with a minimum per trade.

{{ term("sgx-fees", "SGX's fees") }}: a clearing fee of 0.0325% and a trading access fee of 0.0075%, so about $4 on a $10,000 trade.

9% GST on the commission and on SGX's fees.

2. Why small trades cost the most

The minimum fee is what catches beginners. With a $10 minimum, a $500 buy costs $10: 2% gone before the price moves at all. The same $10 on a $5,000 buy is only 0.2%.

And you pay again when you sell. Buying and selling a $500 position costs about 4% in total, about as much as a year of dividends on many Singapore shares.

3. The fees you don't see on the trade

Some brokers also charge for holding your shares, for each dividend they pass on, or for moving your shares out. An ETF takes a small yearly fee from inside the fund, shown as its expense ratio. And buying shares in another currency usually costs a currency-exchange spread.

None of these is a reason not to invest. They're a reason to trade less often and in bigger amounts, and to read your broker's fee page once.
