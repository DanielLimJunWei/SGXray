The ground rules

Using SGXray means you agree to these. They're short, and in plain English on purpose.

Education, not advice

SGXray explains numbers and teaches the basics. It doesn't tell you what to buy, sell or hold, and nothing on it is a recommendation or financial advice. SGXray isn't a licensed financial adviser and isn't regulated by the Monetary Authority of Singapore. Every decision about your money is yours: if you need advice, speak to a licensed financial adviser. Past returns don't predict future ones, and investing can lose money.

How the numbers are made

Prices are each stock's closing price on SGX, with dividends, from {{ price_source }}. SGXray fetches the close once the market has shut, never a price still moving during the day, and every trading day it re-checks the past week's closes, so a correction made after the fact is picked up. Splits and bonus issues are allowed for, so a price from years ago compares fairly with today's. If a change ever looks too big to be a correction, SGXray keeps the figure it had until a person has checked it, rather than letting a glitch move your numbers. Money is worked out to the cent, with half a cent rounded up.

Prices are end of day, not live: during trading hours you see the last close. Your figures are only as accurate as the trades you enter, and forecasts assume the past repeats, which it often doesn't. For tax, legal records or a sale, your broker's and CDP's statements are the official record. The practice portfolio uses pretend money: no real trade ever happens on SGXray.
